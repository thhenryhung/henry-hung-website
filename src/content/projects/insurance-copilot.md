decisions:
  - title: "Response confidence over suggestion frequency"
    chose: "Setting a response-confidence threshold so the co-pilot surfaced only higher-confidence recommendations, then letting relationship managers dismiss or rate them."
    over: "Maximizing suggestion frequency by showing every possible trigger and asking relationship managers to separate meaningful opportunities from noise."
    because: "The tradeoff produced a smaller set of credible recommendations, increasing suggestion acceptance by thirty percentage points while building greater user trust and adoption."
  - title: "LLM-assisted priority over manual triage"
    chose: "A ranked queue in which the co-pilot weighs several customer signals, then explains both the recommendation and the evidence behind it."
    over: "Asking each relationship manager to review every open relationship and decide whom to contact first using judgment alone."
    because: "No single rule captured priority. The LLM combined several signals, while explanations made its reasoning inspectable. The tradeoff was inconsistent rankings and initial skepticism from relationship managers."
  - title: "Draft-and-approve over fully automated replies"
    chose: "The co-pilot drafts a reply for the relationship manager to review and send; it never sends on its own."
    over: "A genuinely considered version that sent AI-generated outreach without human review."
    because: "Review protected accuracy and compliance, preserved relationship nuance and kept relationship managers in control—critical to adoption."
outcome: "Prototype testing increased suggestion acceptance by thirty percentage points, while outreach preparation fell from roughly thirty minutes to under five. The business case identified $35M in projected benefits from greater customer-facing time and lower administrative costs, securing C-suite funding to scale the product.\n\nNext, I would start with fewer, higher-value triggers before expanding recommendations."
